# SALTMDB Viewer UI — Design Research & Improvement Plan

Research date: 2026-07-27. Scope: web research only (no repo code was read to produce this document beyond the grounding brief supplied by the requester).

---

## Research Summary

**AI slop / generic AI-UI critique**

1. [Why AI Design Looks Generic (Superdesign, 2026)](https://superdesign.dev/blog/why-ai-design-looks-generic) — Names the mechanism as "distributional convergence": LLMs default to the statistical mode of their training data, so open-ended design prompts reliably produce Inter font, purple-to-indigo gradients (`#6366F1`/`#8B5CF6`/`#A855F7`), glassmorphism, three-rounded-cards-with-icons, and centered hero sections — because those are literally the safest, most "offend no one" choices in the corpus. Its fix isn't better prompt wording, it's separating taste-direction, exploration, and implementation into distinct steps.
2. [Why Every AI-Built Website Looks the Same — Blame Tailwind's Indigo-500 (DEV.to)](https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p) — Traces the specific purple/indigo epidemic to Tailwind UI's `indigo-500` default (chosen by Adam Wathan as a neutral demo placeholder, not an aesthetic statement), which saturated millions of tutorials/templates and became "the" statistical prior for a button color, now self-reinforcing as AI output re-enters training data.
3. [AI Slop Design: Why AI-Generated UI Looks Generic (VibeCodeKit)](https://vibecodekit.dev/ai-slop-design) and the search-aggregated fingerprint across multiple 2026 sources — the concrete "tell" checklist: Inter-everywhere, lavender accent, glassmorphism-on-everything, badge-above-headline, colored-left-border cards, numbered 1-2-3 steps, dark-mode-by-default with neon cyan/violet glow borders, floating gradient "orbs." A pattern fresh in early 2025 is cliché by mid-2026 purely because the feedback loop digests it fast.
4. [Glassmorphism: The Most Beautiful Trap in Modern UI Design (Medium/Bootcamp)](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/glassmorphism-the-most-beautiful-trap-in-modern-ui-design-a472818a7c0a) and the aggregated 2026 trend-fatigue coverage — glassmorphism's overuse mirrors the skeuomorphism backlash of the early 2010s: it started as a deliberate flourish (Apple's Big Sur/visionOS use), became a copy-pasted default in dashboards and crypto wallets, and now causes real accessibility problems (low text contrast over blurred backgrounds, motion-sickness-adjacent blur, GPU/battery cost from `backdrop-filter` at high blur radii on low-power devices). The live conclusion in current discourse: judge blur/translucency by whether it clarifies depth in *this specific place*, not as a blanket style.
5. [Stop Gratuitous UI Animation (Medium/D-UX)](https://medium.com/@sophie_paxtonUX/stop-gratuitous-ui-animation-9ece9aa9eb97) and related coverage — auto-playing decorative motion (hover glows, floating gradient orbs, pulsing borders) measurably increases cognitive load and can actively block task completion (one cited case: 64% of users missed critical info on a page with animated background graphics). Motion should only ever encode a *state change* (something loaded, something is now selected, something moved in the graph) — never run as ambient decoration.

**Reference dashboards / developer-tool design language**

6. [Linear design tokens (awesome-design-md / DESIGN.md)](https://github.com/voltagent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/linear.app/DESIGN.md) — Concrete, reverse-engineered token set: canvas `#010102` (not pure black — has a deliberate blue tint), a 4-step surface ladder instead of shadows, hairline borders (`#23252a`) as the primary depth cue, one single lavender accent (`#5e6ad2`) reserved *only* for brand mark / primary CTA / focus ring / link emphasis (explicitly: never as a background fill, never a second chromatic accent), Inter-based type at controlled weights (display 600 / body 400, never 700+), negative letter-spacing on display sizes, and an 8/12/24/96 spacing rhythm. Explicit "don't" list: no atmospheric gradients, no spotlight cards, no pill-round CTAs, no light-mode marketing page bolted onto a dark product.
7. [Vercel Geist design system](https://vercel.com/geist/introduction) / [design token breakdown (getdesign.md)](https://getdesign.md/vercel/design-md) / [shadcn Vercel breakdown](https://www.shadcn.io/design/vercel) — "Ink IS the brand": near-white `#fafafa` canvas, near-black `#171717` ink, a 200-step neutral gray scale where *every* border/divider/disabled-state gets its own deliberate step, zero brand-blue accent. 40 color tokens, 15 type styles across Geist Sans + Geist Mono, 9 corner radii, 12 spacing values. The discipline is what's being sold: no second accent, no display weight above 600, no gradient anywhere.
8. [Raycast design tokens (awesome-design-md)](https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/raycast/DESIGN.md) and [A Fresh Look and Feel (Raycast blog)](https://www.raycast.com/blog/a-fresh-look-and-feel) — dark-only by explicit design decision (no light mode at all), a 4-step near-black surface ladder (`#07080a` → `#121212`) with **zero drop shadows anywhere** — depth is built entirely from the surface ladder plus one subtle internal gradient reserved for keycaps. Saturated accent colors (yellow/red/green/blue) are quarantined to extension icons/illustrations and explicitly forbidden on chrome/buttons/text. Inter with OpenType features (`ss03` stylistic alternate) enabled globally as a literal brand signature.
9. [Design System Analysis: PostHog (getdesign.md)](https://getdesign.md/posthog/design-md) and aggregated 2025/2026 SaaS-dashboard survey coverage — PostHog is cited as a working example of "pack a lot of analytics into a dark UI while keeping charts/tables legible," using a single accent (green) sparingly as a wayfinding color rather than a decorative one, which is the opposite instinct from bento-grid gradient dashboards.
10. [Arc Browser design analysis (Blake Crosley) / multiple UX teardowns](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/arc) — Arc's differentiation isn't a visual trend, it's *structural* rethinking (vertical tabs, Spaces, Split View) with restrained, muted color and serif/sans pairing used sparingly for personality, not saturation-as-decoration. Relevant lesson for SALTMDB: "feels crafted" often comes from information-architecture decisions (how the graph/relations are organized and switched between), not from surface treatment.

**Typography / color / layout for data-dense dashboards**

11. [Typography Best Practices for Data-Dense Displays (Dev3lop)](https://dev3lop.com/typography-best-practices-for-data-dense-displays/) and [Best Fonts for Dense Dashboards (FontAlternatives)](https://fontalternatives.com/blog/best-fonts-for-dense-dashboards/) — tabular (lining) numerals in a proportional neo-grotesque give better column alignment and readability than full monospace for numeric data; monospace should be reserved for code/IDs/hashes, not general dashboard numbers. High-x-height neo-grotesks (Switzer cited specifically, also applies to Inter/Geist Sans/system-ui stacks) pack more legible information into less vertical space — directly relevant to a 12-column bento grid trying to show many entities/tags/stats at once.
12. [Semantic colors in design systems (Webheads United)](https://webheadsunited.com/guide-to-semantic-colors-in-design-systems/) and aggregated 2025/2026 dashboard-color-guide coverage — the load-bearing rule: **never reuse status colors (red/amber/green) decoratively.** If red/green/amber appear anywhere as brand color, gradient stops, or category tinting, they stop meaning "error/warning/success" the moment they're needed for that job. A dashboard showing entity types, tags, and relation predicates should use a *separate*, desaturated categorical palette from whatever (if anything) encodes health/error/staleness.
13. Dark-mode-first developer tooling convention (cross-referenced across PostHog/Linear/Raycast/Vercel sources above, and general 2025/2026 dark-dashboard guidance) — background should be a near-black with a deliberate hue tint (blue-black, or warm charcoal), never pure `#000000` (crushes the surface ladder / elevation signal) and never pure white text (`#ffffff)` on it (halation/glare) — off-white (`#e5e7eb`-class) ink is the consistent convention across every reference system above.

**Force-directed graph / node-link visualization**

14. [Force-Directed Graph Layouts — design principles (FasterCapital)](https://fastercapital.com/content/Visualization-Techniques--Force-Directed-Graphs---Revealing-Network-Dynamics-with-Force-Directed-Graphs.html) — the two governing rules of a legible force layout: (a) connected nodes should be pulled close, (b) unconnected nodes should be pushed apart aggressively enough to avoid an unreadable hairball. Edge clutter is the dominant failure mode in dense graphs; edge bundling (routing many edges through shared curve corridors) and **on-demand labeling** (labels appear only on hover/select, not rendered at rest for every node) are the two standard mitigations.
15. [Neo4j Bloom visualization conventions (Neo4j docs + Medium developer blog)](https://medium.com/neo4j/using-bloom-to-visualize-graph-data-scoobygraph-part-3-efb69523d6f8) — the two standard encoding channels in professional graph tools are **node size = a quantitative property** (e.g., degree/PageRank/mention-count) and **node color = category** (entity label/type), configured declaratively per "perspective." This size/color split is the convention SALTMDB's graph view should adopt rather than uniform-size nodes.
16. [Cytoscape.js vs vis-network vs Sigma.js decision guide (PkgPulse, 2026)](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/cytoscape-vs-vis-network-vs-sigma-graph-visualization-2026) and library comparison threads — practical 2026 rule of thumb: **d3-force** for full creative/custom control over a bespoke look, **Cytoscape.js** when you want built-in graph algorithms (centrality, clustering) and analytical interactivity, **Sigma.js** only once graphs are large enough that WebGL rendering matters. For SALTMDB's scale (a personal/team memory graph, realistically hundreds not hundreds-of-thousands of nodes), this points at **d3-force as a standalone physics/layout module** (not the full D3 ecosystem) rather than a full graph-analytics library or a WebGL renderer — see plan below.

**No-build / dependency-light architecture**

17. [The No-Build Manifesto (Blake Crosley)](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/no-build-manifesto) — a working production example of a zero-bundler, zero-Node-toolchain site achieving perfect Lighthouse scores via server-rendered HTML fragments + small hand-written vanilla-JS modules (his own convention: 130–450 lines per interactive component, each an IIFE, no shared client state) + CSS custom properties standing in for a Sass/token-build step. His explicit failure condition — "breaks down past ~5 developers sharing component interfaces, or complex client state" — does **not** apply to SALTMDB's single-maintainer, server-rendered-JSON-polling viewer, which is squarely in the regime this pattern is built for.
18. [vanillaTemplates (GitHub)](https://github.com/Tehes/vanillaTemplates) — representative example of the "HTML-first template engine, zero build step" pattern: valid HTML + `data-*` attributes for binding/loops/conditionals/includes, single DOM-walk render, no virtual DOM, usable directly in-browser or ahead-of-time. Demonstrates that "break the 1700-line string into components" does not require adopting a JS framework — `<template>` elements + a tiny (~100-line) hand-rolled renderer is a well-trodden zero-dependency pattern.

## Current SALTMDB State

The Viewer UI is a genuinely zero-dependency, zero-build tool: a Python `http.server`-based backend (`src/saltmdb/viewer/server.py`, `src/saltmdb/viewer/routes.py`) exposes plain JSON endpoints (`get_entities`, `get_tags`, `get_relations_graph`, `get_lineage`, `get_stats`, `get_search`), and the entire client — HTML, CSS, and vanilla JS — lives as one ~1728-line Python string in `src/saltmdb/viewer/templates.py`, fetched/polled by the browser with no framework, no npm, no bundler. Visually, it currently presents as a 12-column "bento grid" of glassmorphism cards (translucent fills, soft "glass-border" outlines, gradient accents), an SVG donut chart for aggregate stats, and a hand-rolled SVG force-directed graph (manual physics, no D3/Cytoscape/Sigma) for the relation/knowledge graph. This is architecturally sound — the no-dependency stance is worth preserving — but the *visual language on top of that architecture* is, by the requester's own description, reading as generic "AI slop": glassmorphism-as-default, gradient accents, bento-card layout — precisely the fingerprint described in sources 1–5 above.

## Gaps / Problems

1. **Glassmorphism as the default surface, not a deliberate exception.** Source 4 is unambiguous: translucent-blur cards read as "in 2021, cliché by 2025/2026" and actively hurt contrast/legibility for a text- and data-heavy tool. A memory-graph dashboard is read closely (titles, tags, relation predicates) — this is exactly the content type glassmorphism actively degrades (low text contrast over blurred backgrounds), not a hero marketing panel where a little blur is harmless.
2. **Gradient accents are the single loudest "AI-generated" tell in 2026** (sources 1–3), and SALTMDB's stated visual style explicitly includes "gradient accents." Whatever hex values are currently in use, if they trend toward indigo/violet/purple they are hitting the exact Tailwind-`indigo-500`-derived fingerprint the research repeatedly flags as the #1 giveaway.
3. **Bento grid + donut chart is a decorative-density mismatch.** Bento layouts optimize for *visual variety* (marketing/portfolio sites), not for *information density* (source 11: dashboard users want data, not breathing room, but with *intentional* tight-but-consistent spacing — not whitespace-for-its-own-sake card padding). A donut chart in particular is a low-information-density chart type (one or two comparisons max) being used to represent what is likely a multi-category tag/entity-type breakdown better served by a sorted bar list or table.
4. **No stated color discipline: decorative vs. semantic collision risk.** Source 12 is explicit: if status/health colors (e.g., for a stale/broken relation, an orphaned entity, a consolidation-pending flag) share a palette with purely decorative category colors (tag colors, entity-type colors), the semantic colors lose their signal value. Nothing in the current description suggests SALTMDB's color system separates "this red means something is wrong" from "this red is just this tag's assigned color."
5. **Hand-rolled SVG force graph has no stated legibility controls.** Sources 14–15 identify the two concrete failure modes of amateur force-directed graphs: (a) no edge-clutter mitigation (labels rendered at rest for every node → hairball at even moderate graph sizes) and (b) uniform node size/color instead of size-encodes-a-quantity + color-encodes-category. The brief doesn't mention SALTMDB's implementation having either — worth auditing, but the described "manual physics" build-from-scratch approach is a red flag for missing tuned repulsion/link-distance constants that real force libraries ship with defaults for.
6. **Single 1728-line template string is a template debt problem independent of visual design.** It's not "wrong" per the no-build philosophy (source 17 endorses no-bundler entirely) — but 1728 lines in one Python string with no component boundaries is past the point where even Crosley's own no-build convention (130–450 lines *per component*) would recommend splitting, and it makes iterating on the visual redesign itself higher-risk (every CSS/JS change touches the same monolithic string with no isolation).
7. **No evidence of a dark-mode-first, restrained-palette approach**, despite that being the unanimous convention across every developer-tool reference studied (Linear, Raycast, Vercel, PostHog — sources 6–9, 13). A glassmorphism-plus-gradient aesthetic is trend-chasing in the *opposite* direction from where every credible 2025/2026 dev-tool design reference has converged: a disciplined near-black (not pure-black) canvas, a hairline-border-driven surface ladder instead of blur/shadow, and exactly one accent color used sparingly and only for interactive/brand moments.

## Proposed Improvement Plan

### Phase 1 — Quick Wins (CSS/token-only, low risk, no JS structural change)

All of these can land as edits inside the existing `<style>` block in `templates.py` without touching the JS or the Python routing layer.

1. **Kill glassmorphism as the default card treatment.** Remove `backdrop-filter: blur(...)` and translucent `rgba` fills from the base `.card`/`.glass-border` classes. Replace with the Linear/Raycast pattern: opaque surface steps (`--surface-0`, `--surface-1`, `--surface-2`, `--surface-3`) differentiated by lightness alone, plus 1px hairline borders (`--border-hairline`) for separation. Reserve any blur (if kept at all) for exactly one place — e.g. a command palette / modal overlay — never for data cards.
2. **Replace the gradient accent with a single flat accent color**, chosen deliberately (not indigo/violet — sources 1–2 flag that hue specifically as the tell). Given SALTMDB's brand-neutral status, pick from an underused-in-AI-slop family: a desaturated teal, amber, or slate-blue. Use it in exactly the reserved slots Linear/Raycast define: primary CTA, focus ring, active nav state, link emphasis — never as a card background or a full-bleed hero gradient.
3. **Introduce a real design-token layer via CSS custom properties** on `:root` (and a `[data-theme="light"]` override block if light mode is kept) — colors, spacing scale (4px base: 4/8/12/16/24/32/48/96, matching the Linear/Raycast convention in sources 6/8), radii (4/6/8/12/16px, not one blanket `border-radius: 16px` everywhere), and type scale. This is pure CSS, zero new JS, and immediately makes every subsequent visual change a token edit instead of a hunt-and-replace across 1700 lines.
4. **Fix numeral rendering for stats**: apply `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` to any numeric cell/stat (counts, IDs, timestamps) instead of relying on default proportional figures — directly actionable per source 11, zero risk, one CSS line.
5. **Separate semantic color from categorical color explicitly.** Define `--status-error`, `--status-warning`, `--status-ok` as a small reserved triad (red/amber/green) used *only* for health/staleness/error states (orphaned entities, failed consolidation, broken relation links) — and give tag/entity-type category coloring its own separate, desaturated palette that never overlaps those three hues.
6. **Replace the SVG donut chart** with a horizontal sorted bar list or a simple stat-tile row for aggregate stats (entity counts by type, tag frequency) — donuts are low-information-density and rarely the right call for more than 2–3 categories (source 11's density argument applies here too).
7. **Kill any ambient/decorative motion** (pulsing glows, floating gradient orbs, animated card entrances on load) per source 5 — motion should only fire on a real state transition (data refreshed, node selected, search result count changed).

### Phase 2 — Medium-Term (structural template/JS changes, moderate risk)

8. **Break `templates.py`'s single HTML string into `<template>`-element partials rendered by a small (~100–150 line) hand-rolled vanilla-JS renderer**, following the pattern in source 18 (`vanillaTemplates`) — not by adopting that library specifically, but by writing an equivalent tiny binder: HTML `<template>` blocks for each major panel (entity list, tag cloud, stats panel, graph panel, search results), plus `data-bind`/`data-if`/`data-for` attributes and one small render function that walks bound elements once per data update. This keeps the "one file, no build step" property (the string can still live in `templates.py`, or split into a handful of `.html` template-literal constants alongside it) while giving each panel real component boundaries — directly addressing the 1728-line monolith problem (Gap 6) without violating the zero-dependency constraint (source 17's own convention explicitly endorses per-component file/section splitting under a no-build regime).
9. **Rework the bento grid into a density-first layout**: a fixed-ratio two/three-pane layout (nav/filter rail, primary content, detail/inspector panel — the layout convention shared by Linear, Grafana, and PostHog) rather than a variable-span card grid. Bento's variable card sizing is a *marketing-page* pattern optimized for visual rhythm, not for scanning a list of entities/tags/relations quickly.
10. **Add on-demand labeling and degree-based node sizing to the force graph** (source 14, 15): render node labels only on hover/selection (not at rest for every node past some node-count threshold), size nodes by relation-degree or mention count, and color nodes by entity/tag category using the same categorical palette introduced in Quick Win 5 — all achievable inside the existing hand-rolled SVG physics sim without swapping libraries.
11. **Add edge decluttering controls**: a simple "minimum relation count to show" or "collapse to N-hop from selected node" threshold control (a checkbox/slider in the graph panel), which is the standard mitigation for the hairball failure mode in dense graphs (source 14) and is pure JS/state logic, no new dependency.

### Phase 3 — Larger Bets (bigger structural change, needs explicit buy-in)

12. **Vendor `d3-force` as a single dependency-light physics module** (not the full D3 bundle — `d3-force` alone is ~20KB unminified, no build step required, can be dropped in as a single `<script>` vendored file or CDN-pinned with SRI hash) to replace the hand-rolled physics loop, while keeping SALTMDB's own hand-rolled SVG rendering/interaction layer on top. This is the single highest-leverage swap per source 16's decision guide: d3-force is explicitly the "full creative control, custom look" choice — appropriate here because SALTMDB wants a bespoke rendering style, not Cytoscape's analytics UI or Sigma's WebGL scale. This is the one recommendation in this document that **does** cross the "truly zero dependency" line — see Risks below; it should be treated as opt-in and reversible (kept in one isolated JS module so it can be ripped out without touching the rest of the template).
13. **Formalize the token system into a small theme file** (`templates.py` could grow a `TOKENS_CSS` constant, or — if the project is willing to add a build step of exactly zero complexity — a static `.css` file served alongside the HTML) so color/spacing/type tokens are edited in one place and could eventually support the dark/light toggle cleanly, matching how every reference system studied (Linear, Vercel Geist, Raycast) treats tokens as the single source of truth rather than scattered literal values.
14. **Reconsider whether light mode is worth supporting at all.** Raycast's explicit stance (source 8: "don't introduce a light mode, the system is dark-only by design") is a legitimate option for a niche developer tool — every reference system in this research that targets developers-at-a-terminal (Raycast, Linear's product surface, Vercel dashboards) treats dark as the *native* medium, not a toggle. Dropping light-mode support entirely would simplify the token system and let every color decision be made once.

## Risks & Trade-offs

- **Zero-dependency identity vs. d3-force (Phase 3, item 12).** This is the one recommendation that visibly breaks "no dependencies, no build step, no JS framework." It's a real tension, not a minor caveat: adding *any* vendored JS file, even a small single-purpose physics module with no transitive dependencies, is a philosophy change, and should be a decision zbalint makes explicitly rather than something slipped in as a refactor. The mitigating case: `d3-force` is a physics *algorithm* library, not a rendering framework — it computes positions, SALTMDB's own SVG code still does 100% of the rendering/interaction — so it's a much smaller philosophical breach than adopting Cytoscape.js/Sigma.js (which own rendering, canvas/WebGL context, and often bring their own UI chrome). If the answer is "no, stay pure," the fallback is: keep the hand-rolled sim, but explicitly tune its constants against source 14's two governing rules (adequate repulsion, link-distance proportional to weight) and add the labeling/sizing/decluttering wins from Phase 2 — those alone close most of the legibility gap without any dependency.
- **Dark-mode-only (item 14) is a real feature cut**, not free — some users/embeds may expect a light theme, and if SALTMDB is ever screenshotted/embedded in light-themed docs or shared dashboards, dropping light mode forecloses that. This should be validated against how SALTMDB's viewer is actually used (personal local tool vs. something screenshared/embedded) before committing.
- **Splitting `templates.py` into `<template>`-partial + tiny renderer (Phase 2, item 8) adds a small amount of indirection** for a currently very simple "one big string, `.format()`/f-string it, ship it" model. It's the right call for maintainability at 1728 lines, but it's genuine new code (a ~150-line renderer) that has to be gotten right and tested — not a pure subtraction of complexity, a relocation of it into a more maintainable shape.
- **Removing glassmorphism/gradients is subjective taste, dressed as research-backed critique.** The sourced critique (sources 1–5) is real and current, but "glassmorphism is over" is itself a trend statement that could look dated in another 2–3 years the way skeuomorphism did. The token-system approach (Quick Win 3) is deliberately future-proofed against this: if the aesthetic verdict changes later, it's a token-value edit, not a rearchitecture.
- **Bar chart / stat-tile replacing the donut chart (Quick Win 6) is a minor loss of "at a glance" visual appeal** for a single high-level ratio (e.g., "70% of entities are memories, 30% events") where a donut genuinely does communicate a single proportion faster than a bar list — the tradeoff should be decided per-metric, not blanket-applied to every chart in the stats panel.

---

## Open Questions for zbalint

1. Is dark-mode-only (dropping light theme support entirely, per Raycast's stance) acceptable, or does the viewer need to stay usable in light-themed contexts (screenshots, embeds, docs)?
2. Is vendoring a single dependency-light physics module (`d3-force`, ~20KB, no transitive deps, rendering stays hand-rolled) an acceptable one-time exception to the zero-dependency policy, or is "truly zero JS dependencies, no exceptions" a hard line?
3. What's the realistic upper bound on graph size (entity + relation count) the viewer needs to stay legible at? This determines how much of Phase 2's edge-decluttering/labeling work is necessary vs. premature — a graph that never exceeds ~150 nodes needs much less clutter mitigation than one that could hit thousands.
4. Should the stats panel prioritize a small number of hero metrics (Linear/Vercel style: a few big numbers, high restraint) or a denser table/grid of many metrics (Grafana style: maximum information per screen)? This materially changes the Phase 1/2 layout decisions.
5. Is there an existing brand color anywhere else in the SALTMDB ecosystem (docs, README, other tooling) that the new accent color should match, or is the viewer free to establish its own from scratch?
